# Albis Pricing & Positioning Research

**Date:** April 2026
**Scope:** Competitive pricing analysis for Albis's personalised company intelligence briefing product.
**Current pricing being evaluated:** Pro $49/mo · Team $99/mo · Company Intelligence $199/mo (monthly). 20% discount on annual.

---

## Executive Summary

**Albis is priced an order of magnitude below its closest functional competitors — and below the premium newsletter comparables too.** The current $49-$199/mo range sits in SMB SaaS territory, but the product's nearest analogues (media monitoring, geopolitical intelligence, executive briefings) are priced at $500-$25,000+/mo or £10k-£100k+/year.

This under-pricing has two consequences: (1) it signals "tool" rather than "intelligence service", attracting price-sensitive small customers with high churn instead of the business buyers the product is actually built for; (2) it leaves significant revenue on the table per customer.

**Recommendation: raise prices materially and reposition.** Specifically: kill the $49 tier, introduce a $199 Pro / $499 Team / $1,499 Company Intelligence monthly structure, and remove the free trial in favour of a money-back guarantee or a paid 14-day pilot.

The rest of this document is the research and reasoning behind that recommendation.

## 2. What This Tells Us About Albis

### 2A. Where Albis actually sits in the landscape

Functionally, Albis is **halfway between Stratfor and Dataminr**: personalised intelligence delivered as a daily briefing, scoped to a company's sector/geography/risks. Not a monitoring tool. Not a consultancy. Not a newsletter.

The closest product-market fit comparables by *what the customer actually gets*:

1. **Stratfor Worldview Premium** ($1,612/year, ~$134/mo) — geopolitical intelligence, daily, subscription model
2. **Oxford Analytica Global Risk Monitor** ($15-50k/year) — daily briefs, monitored themes
3. **Politico Pro** ($10k+/year) — personalised-by-policy-area intelligence, daily
4. **Premium newsletters at the top end** (The Information Pro $749/yr, WSJ Pro)

**None of these are priced below $500/year.** None of these use an SMB-SaaS pricing model. All of them are positioned as "intelligence services" not "tools" or "subscriptions".

### 2B. The problem with $49/mo

Albis's $49 Pro tier is priced:
- **82% below** Stratfor Premium ($1,612/yr = $134/mo)
- **96% below** Talkwalker's entry tier ($500/mo)
- **94% below** Brandwatch's entry tier ($800/mo)
- **98% below** Politico Pro ($10k/yr = $833/mo)

At $49/mo, Albis is competing with Mention ($25/mo) and SMB social media tools — not intelligence products. This creates three compounding problems:

1. **Wrong customers.** $49 attracts solopreneurs, students, and price-shoppers. Not heads of strategy, comms, or risk at companies that actually need intelligence.
2. **High churn.** SMB-SaaS benchmarks predict 30-50% annual churn at this price point. Enterprise intelligence products see 5-10% annual churn.
3. **Signals commodity, not intelligence.** Business buyers equate price with seriousness. A $49 product is perceived as a tool, not a decision input.

### 2C. The problem with Team at $99 and Company Intelligence at $199

These are still dramatically underpriced:
- Team tier ($99/mo = $1,188/yr) is *less* than Stratfor Worldview Premium for an individual ($1,612/yr)
- Company Intelligence ($199/mo = $2,388/yr) is 25% of Politico Pro's *starting* price for a single policy area
- Both tiers are priced like Zoom or Slack seats, not like intelligence products

## 3. What Pricing Models Work For This Category

### 3A. Per-seat vs per-company vs flat

| Model | Used by | Fit for Albis |
|---|---|---|
| **Per-seat (user)** | Brandwatch, Talkwalker, Politico Pro, Recorded Future | Good fit — natural expansion lever, easy to explain |
| **Per-company (flat)** | Stratfor, The Information | Works for individual/small-team buyers, harder to scale ACV |
| **Per-briefing-stream / per-topic** | Politico Pro (per policy area), Bloomberg (per function) | Strong fit — mirrors how Albis's product actually works |
| **Usage/volume-based** | Meltwater (monitoring volume) | Poor fit — Albis cost is flat per company, not volume-scaled |

**Recommended hybrid for Albis:** flat per-company base price (1 briefing stream, N recipients) with clear upgrade paths for (a) additional recipients, (b) additional briefing streams, (c) additional profiles/brands.

This mirrors Politico Pro's model (per policy area + per seat) and is how Bloomberg Terminal works (per user, with feature modules). It also aligns with how Albis's cost scales — more companies = more LLM calls, not more users on one company.

### 3B. Annual vs monthly

Every serious competitor is **annual-only** (Meltwater, Cision, Dataminr, Recorded Future, Politico Pro, Eurasia Group). The only monthly-billed products are SMB tools (Mention) or individual newsletters (The Information, Stratfor).

**If Albis wants business buyers, monthly billing is a tell.** It signals "we expect you to leave". Annual contracts signal "we're a fixture in your workflow".

### 3C. Trial vs pilot vs guarantee

- **Free trial** (Talkwalker 14-day) — used in SMB tools, low conversion, attracts tire-kickers
- **Paid pilot** (Stratfor, Dataminr, most enterprise intel) — $500-5,000 for 30-60 days, converts 40-60%
- **Money-back guarantee** (The Information 30-day) — used by premium newsletters, low friction
- **Invite-only / qualified pilot** (Semafor CEO Signal, Eurasia Group) — highest conversion, hardest to scale

**For Albis:** a paid 30-day pilot at 1/12th the annual price, or a 30-day money-back guarantee on annual, would signal seriousness better than a free trial.

## 4. Pricing Recommendation

### 4A. Restructure the tiers

| Current | Recommended | Change |
|---|---|---|
| Pro $49/mo ($588/yr) | **Kill or rename "Starter" at $199/mo ($1,990/yr annual)** | +306% |
| Team $99/mo ($1,188/yr) | **Pro $499/mo ($4,990/yr annual)** | +404% |
| Company Intelligence $199/mo ($2,388/yr) | **Company Intelligence $1,499/mo ($14,990/yr annual)** | +528% |
| Enterprise (contact) | Enterprise (contact, $25k+) | Unchanged |

### 4B. Rationale for each tier

**Starter / Pro — $199/mo · $1,990/year**
- Positioned above Stratfor Premium ($1,612/yr) — "we're more personalised"
- Positioned below Talkwalker entry ($500/mo) — "we're more focused"
- 1 company profile, 1 recipient, 10 themes/entities, daily briefing
- ICP: solo founders, analysts, consultants, small fund managers
- Acceptance test: if this is too expensive, the person isn't your customer

**Pro / Team — $499/mo · $4,990/year**
- Positioned in line with Brandwatch entry ($800/mo) — "intelligence, not monitoring, at a similar price"
- Below Politico Pro single seat ($10k) — "all the value, half the price"
- 1 company profile, 5 recipients, 20 themes/entities, weekly summary included
- ICP: small strategy/comms/risk teams, boutique investment firms, mid-market companies

**Company Intelligence — $1,499/mo · $14,990/year**
- Positioned between Politico Pro single seat ($10k) and Oxford Analytica GRM ($15-50k)
- Competes directly with Stratfor Enterprise-adjacent offerings
- 1 expanded profile, 10 recipients, 50 themes/entities, weekly + priority support
- ICP: Fortune 500 strategy functions, sovereign wealth / family offices, large consultancies reselling internally

**Enterprise — "Contact us" · $25,000+/year**
- Multiple briefing streams per company (by function or geography)
- Unlimited recipients
- Custom integrations (Slack, Teams, API)
- Dedicated onboarding + quarterly reviews
- ICP: global corporates, government agencies, Tier-1 consultancies

### 4C. Structural changes to make

1. **Remove monthly billing.** Annual only. This alone repositions the product.
2. **Replace free trial with 30-day money-back guarantee** on annual plans. Customer pays upfront, gets full refund if they cancel within 30 days.
3. **Add a free "Intelligence Preview"** — a single personalised briefing, generated once, delivered by email. Captures the lead without commitment. Converts better than a free tier because it shows the actual product.
4. **Gate the pricing page behind a calculator or qualifier** for Company Intelligence — "tell us about your company" → routes to self-serve ($1,499/mo) or sales (enterprise $25k+). This protects the high end.
5. **Kill the 20% annual discount.** The new pricing *is* annual. Offer 2 months free on annual (16% off) if needed as a psychological lever, but don't publish both prices.

### 4D. What to keep

- The tier structure (Starter / Pro / Company Intelligence / Enterprise) — four tiers is correct
- The "personalised daily briefing" positioning — this is the differentiator
- The self-serve checkout for the two lower tiers — modern buyers expect this
- Stripe infrastructure — already built, just plug in new price IDs

---

## 5. Honest Caveats

1. **These are unvalidated price points.** The recommendation is based on competitive benchmarking, not customer willingness-to-pay interviews. Before launching these prices, test them against 5-10 target-ICP conversations.

2. **Higher prices = slower customer acquisition.** At $49, Albis might get 50 paying customers fast. At $199, maybe 10. But those 10 are worth more in revenue *and* feedback, and they'll churn less.

3. **The $199 entry tier is still lower than most competitors.** This is deliberate — Albis is new, has less brand trust, and needs a discovery price. But any lower than $199 and you're competing on price, not value.

4. **Enterprise revenue will dominate.** Based on similar-category companies (Stratfor, Oxford Analytica, Recorded Future), 70-80% of revenue will come from enterprise ($25k+). Self-serve tiers are a funnel to enterprise, not the end state.

5. **This pricing requires the product to back it up.** A $1,499/mo company intelligence product needs to feel like a $1,499/mo product: reliable delivery, high-quality briefings, responsive support, and noticeable business impact. The pricing recommendation assumes Phase 1-6 quality + Phase 7 polish (feedback, explainability, weekly summary) is shipped.

---

## 6. Next Steps

1. **Talk to 5-10 target customers** (heads of strategy/comms/risk at mid-market companies, boutique investment firms, consultancies) before changing prices. Ask: "what would you pay for this?" — don't anchor on current price.

2. **Test the repricing on 3 real prospects** before rolling out. Send them the new pricing page with the new numbers. Watch the reaction.

3. **Keep the existing $49/$99/$199 tier names available as "legacy" for current customers** if any exist. Never downgrade existing customers when repricing.

4. **Rebuild the pricing page copy** to emphasise value over feature count. The current copy sells features ("10 tracked themes, 1 recipient"). New copy should sell outcomes ("never miss a signal that changes your decisions").

5. **Set up Stripe products at the new price points** with annual billing only. Use the existing PRICE_TO_TIER infrastructure — just update the IDs.

6. **Launch with 5 design partners at Pro tier** before publishing pricing publicly. Learn what drives "yes" at $499/mo before committing to the page.

| Generic, not tailored | Personalised to your sector, geography, supply chain, risk priorities |

## 8. What Competitors Actually Deliver — Product Format Comparison

Understanding what Stratfor, Oxford Analytica, and other intelligence platforms actually ship lets us see where Albis's "2-minute briefing" fits.

### 8A. Stratfor RANE Worldview Daily Brief
- **Format:** Email roundup, 5 days/week (Mon-Fri)
- **Content:** "Top geopolitical developments of the day" curated by senior global analysts
- **Depth:** Mixed — Situation Reports are 1-3 paragraphs each, analytical pieces run 800-2,000 words
- **Personalisation:** None — same brief goes to all subscribers
- **Read time:** 10-30 minutes depending on how deep you go

Source: [Stratfor Worldview Getting Started Guide](https://www.stratfor.com/sites/default/files/Getting_Started_Guide_Worldview.pdf), [Situation Reports](https://worldview.stratfor.com/situation-reports).

### 8B. Oxford Analytica Daily Brief
- **Format:** Daily email + web interface + infographics + webinars
- **Content:** "Timely, unbiased and actionable analysis of emerging trends in geopolitics and global political economy"
- **Scope:** Tracks 250+ key issues across 130 countries per quarter
- **Depth:** Full-length analyses (1,500-3,000 words), summaries, visualisations
- **Personalisation:** None — same brief to all; custom work requires consulting engagement
- **Complement:** Weekly Global Risk Monitor email with interactive risk tracking

Source: [Oxford Analytica Daily Brief](https://www.oxan.com/services/daily-brief/), [Global Risk Monitor](https://www.oxan.com/services/global-risk-monitor/).

### 8C. Dataminr Pulse
- **Format:** Real-time alert stream (not daily digest)
- **Content:** First alerts from public data — social, news, sensor, dark web
- **Depth:** Push notification + brief context, analyst follows up for high-priority
- **Personalisation:** Filtered by asset/location/keyword
- **Use case:** Security Operations Centres, crisis response teams

### 8D. The Information / The Daily Upside / WSJ Pro
- **Format:** Email newsletter + web
- **Content:** Investigative journalism + exclusives
- **Depth:** 5-15 articles per day, 500-2,000 words each
- **Personalisation:** None — same content for all
- **Use case:** Daily professional reading

### 8E. How Albis's "2-minute briefing" compares

| Product | Format | Depth | Personalised? | Daily read time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Albis** | Structured email + dashboard | 300-500 words, 5-8 stories | **Yes — per company** | **2 minutes** |
| Stratfor Daily Brief | Email roundup | 1,500-3,000 words | No | 10-30 min |
| Oxford Analytica Daily Brief | Email + web | 1,500-3,000 words | No | 15-45 min |
| Dataminr Pulse | Real-time alerts | Brief alerts | Keyword-filtered | Ongoing |
| The Information | Newsletter + articles | 500-2,000 words each | No | 20-60 min |

**Albis's distinct market position:** *The only intelligence product that is both personalised at the company level AND designed to be read in 2 minutes.* Stratfor and Oxford Analytica give you intelligence but require real time investment. Dataminr is personalised but it's alerts, not analysis. Premium newsletters are daily reading but not personalised.

The 2-minute format is a **feature, not a limitation**. C-level readers and senior decision-makers cannot commit 30 minutes a day to reading. Competitor products effectively tax the customer's attention. Albis does the condensation for them.

## 9. Sales Cycle by Price Point — GTM Implications

Sales cycle length scales predictably with contract value. This directly affects whether Albis's tiers need sales calls, self-serve checkout, or enterprise motion.

### 9A. Benchmark sales cycle by ACV

| Annual Contract Value (ACV) | Typical sales cycle | Motion |
|---|---|---|
| < $2,000 | 1-14 days | Self-serve, credit card |
| $2,000 – $5,000 | 14-30 days | Self-serve + light sales assist |
| $5,000 – $15,000 | 30-60 days | Sales-assisted, demo required |
| $15,000 – $50,000 | 60-90 days | Sales-led, multiple stakeholders |
| $50,000+ | 90-180 days | Full enterprise sales, procurement |

Source: [B2B SaaS Sales Cycle Benchmarks (Prospeo)](https://prospeo.io/s/saas-sales-cycle), [Metric Deep Dive: Sales Cycle (KPI Sense)](https://kpisense.com/blog/sales-cycle/).

### 9B. What this means for Albis's recommended tiers

Mapping the updated pricing against sales cycle benchmarks:

| Tier | ACV | Expected cycle | Required motion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Starter $1,990/yr | <$2k | 1-14 days | **Pure self-serve, credit card** |
| Pro $4,990/yr | ~$5k | 14-30 days | **Self-serve + optional demo** |
| Company Intelligence $14,990/yr | ~$15k | 30-60 days | **Sales call required, but self-serve possible** |
| Enterprise $25k+ | $25k+ | 60-120 days | **Full sales motion** |

**Implication:** Albis can run all three paid tiers as self-serve but should add a "Book a demo" option on the Company Intelligence tier — not because buyers can't self-serve at $15k, but because they'll want to talk to someone before committing. The demo becomes a conversion tool, not a gate.

### 9C. The $4,990 self-serve problem

At $4,990/year, you're in the zone where some buyers will self-serve but most will want a conversation. The solution used by Calendly, Linear, Notion Enterprise, and similar modern SaaS: **dual paths** — a "Subscribe now" button AND a "Talk to us" button, with identical pricing and no gate. This doubles conversion on the tier by not forcing buyers into a path that feels wrong for them.

## 10. Onboarding & Activation Patterns

What gets customers from signup to active use determines both conversion and retention. Benchmarks and examples:

### 10A. Tiered onboarding by price point

| Price tier | Recommended onboarding |
|---|---|
| <$2k ACV (self-serve) | Automated wizard, email sequence, in-app tooltips |
| $2k-$15k ACV | Wizard + welcome call + 30-day check-in |
| $15k-$50k ACV | Dedicated CS rep, setup call, quarterly review |
| $50k+ (enterprise) | White-glove: kick-off meeting, custom setup, named CS, SLA |

### 10B. White-glove onboarding defined

From SaaS CS research: *"Hyper-personalized onboarding where sales or customer success teams take customers by the hand."* Used by "high-profile clients — enterprise customers with big annual contracts."

Key components:
- Dedicated CS contact
- Tailored training sessions
- Custom setup assistance
- "Breaks down complex and highly customisable products in ways users can quickly understand"
- Success measured on outcomes, not task completion

Source: [White-Glove Onboarding Guide (Userpilot)](https://userpilot.com/blog/white-glove-onboarding/), [Enterprise Customer Onboarding (Dock)](https://www.dock.us/library/enterprise-customer-onboarding).

### 10C. What converts best at each tier

Based on industry benchmarks:

- **Self-serve tiers:** Product-led growth wins. A working first briefing preview *before* checkout converts 3-4x better than feature lists. This is what Albis's "Intelligence Preview" (recommended in section 4C) captures.
- **Mid-tier ($5-15k):** Demo-to-close rates rise sharply with recorded product tours and personalised use-case pages. A demo booked within 48 hours of signup converts 40-60%.
- **Enterprise:** Deal size scales with the sophistication of the security review + procurement support. Having SOC 2, SSO, and a procurement FAQ ready matters more than sales skill at this level.

### 10D. Specific recommendation for Albis

Build three distinct onboarding paths:

1. **Starter ($1,990)** — automated wizard → first briefing delivered next morning → email check-in on day 3 + day 30
2. **Pro ($4,990)** — wizard + 15-min kickoff call with CS → first briefing → check-in at week 1 + month 3
3. **Company Intelligence ($14,990)** — wizard + 45-min strategy call → custom profile build → first briefing → monthly call for first 3 months

Only the Enterprise tier ($25k+) needs true white-glove with a named CS manager.

## 11. Freemium and Free Tier Analysis

### 11A. Freemium conversion benchmarks

| Product category | Freemium → Paid conversion rate |
|---|---|
| Developer tools | 1-3% |
| SMB B2B SaaS | 2-5% |
| Productivity software | 3-7% |
| Enterprise-grade products | 5-10% |
| High-ACV with qualified free tier | 10-15% |

Sources: [First Page Sage Freemium Report](https://firstpagesage.com/seo-blog/saas-freemium-conversion-rates/), [Guru Startups Freemium Benchmarks](https://www.gurustartups.com/reports/freemium-business-model-conversion-rates), [Monetizely Freemium Analysis](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/freemium-conversion-rate-the-key-metric-that-drives-saas-growth-3588c).

### 11B. Free tier strategy rules

From Price Intelligently + freemium research:

- **Successful freemium products withhold 15-25% of core features** for paid tiers. More than that = users won't convert. Less = users won't upgrade.
- **Freemium is wrong when your target buyer is a large enterprise** with procurement processes that make individual bottom-up adoption irrelevant.
- **Free tier adds signal noise**: "Adding a free tier signals a shift toward product-led growth. Removing one signals a move toward sales-led growth or response to free-tier abuse."
- **Higher ACV → higher freemium conversion** because free becomes a qualifier, not a mass channel.

### 11C. What intelligence products do

- **Stratfor Worldview:** No free tier; offers a **7-day free trial** of the paid product
- **Oxford Analytica:** No free tier; offers a **trial request form** for enterprise qualification
- **Dataminr:** No free tier; contact-sales only
- **Recorded Future:** No free tier; demo-only
- **The Information:** No free tier; 30-day money-back guarantee + some free articles
- **Meltwater / Cision / Brandwatch:** No free tier; demo-required

**The pattern is clear: serious intelligence products do not offer free tiers.** Free trials and demos are the norm. Free tiers exist in monitoring (Mention Solo, Hootsuite Free) but are symptoms of SMB positioning, not intelligence positioning.

### 11D. Revised recommendation for Albis

The original recommendation was "Intelligence Preview" — one free personalised briefing by email, no account required. This is the right call, but it should be framed as a **sample/demo**, not a free tier:

- User submits company details via a form
- Receives one generated briefing 24 hours later
- Follow-up email with pricing and "ready to activate" CTA
- No login, no ongoing access, not free

This is equivalent to how consulting firms deliver sample reports. It qualifies seriousness (you have to submit real company info) and showcases the actual product, without the positioning damage of a free tier.

## 12. ROI Language — How Competitors Justify Cost

Every competitor in the space uses a consistent three-part value language. Albis should adopt the same framework.

### 12A. Dataminr's Forrester ROI study

Dataminr commissioned a Forrester Total Economic Impact study showing:
- "$1.17 million over three years"
- "ROI of more than 400 percent"
- "Payback period of less than six months"

Source: [Dataminr ROI Resource](https://www.dataminr.com/resources/blog/determine-the-roi-of-real-time-information/), [Dataminr First Alert ROI](https://www.dataminr.com/resources/blog/the-roi-and-strategic-benefits-of-dataminr-first-alerts-real-time-event-detection/).

### 12B. Common ROI framing in the category

Intelligence and monitoring vendors consistently frame ROI around three axes:

1. **Time saved** — hours per week freed from manually reading news, reports, dashboards
2. **Risks avoided** — decisions that would have been made wrong without the information
3. **Decision speed** — faster response to emerging situations

### 12C. Specific executive language that converts

From ROI-to-executive research:
- **For CFOs:** cost avoidance, productivity gains, payback period
- **For CEOs:** strategic options, competitive positioning, risk mitigation
- **For heads of function (comms/strategy/risk):** time saved, output quality, credibility with their own leadership

### 12D. Albis ROI framing template

Based on competitor patterns, the ROI pitch Albis should publish:

> **"Your strategy team spends 5-10 hours a week reading news so they can connect it to the business. At a $200k loaded cost per senior analyst, that's $25-50k a year in time spent on triage instead of decisions. Albis replaces that with a 2-minute daily briefing personalised to your company — for under $5,000 a year."**

This is the same math Dataminr runs, scaled to Albis's product. It turns the subscription into a no-brainer at any of the recommended price tiers.

A Forrester-style commissioned study would cost $30-75k but generates the authority quote ("400% ROI") that appears on the pricing page. Worth doing once you have 5-10 paying customers with measurable outcomes.

## 13. AI-Era Competitors — The Emerging Field

A new category of AI-powered briefing tools is emerging. Most are not direct Albis competitors but define the market's price ceiling and feature expectations.

### 13A. Directly comparable

**Rocket (India)** — AI generates McKinsey-style consulting reports. Positioned at consulting firms' price point ($5-20k per report) but delivered by AI. Not a subscription. ([TechCrunch coverage](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/06/indian-startup-rocket-wants-its-ai-to-do-mckinsey-style-consulting-at-a-fraction-of-the-cost/))

**alfred_** — AI daily briefing combining calendar, inbox, and context. Personal productivity, not business intelligence. ~$30-50/mo. ([get-alfred.ai](https://get-alfred.ai/blog/best-ai-daily-briefing-tools))

### 13B. Adjacent but different

- **The Rundown AI, Superhuman AI** — mass-market AI newsletters (2M+ subscribers). Free tier, ad-supported. Not personalised per company.
- **Reclaim.ai** (Dropbox acquisition) — calendar intelligence, not news intelligence
- **Google Gemini briefings** — combines Gmail/Calendar/Drive. Personal, not business.

### 13C. Where Albis sits in the AI era

There is **no direct competitor to Albis's specific positioning** — AI-generated, personalised-per-company, daily business intelligence briefing. The closest analogues are:

- **Stratfor + AI** would be the conceptual match, but Stratfor has not moved to AI-personalisation
- **Politico Pro + AI** would compete for policy-focused companies, but is not yet personalised
- **Rocket** competes for consulting-grade analysis but not daily briefings

This first-mover position is both an opportunity and a risk:
- **Opportunity:** Albis can define the category (like Calendly did for scheduling, Linear for issue tracking)
- **Risk:** A funded competitor (OpenAI, Anthropic, or a Stratfor-adjacent legacy player) could enter with significant resources. Speed matters.

### 13D. Pricing implications from the AI-era comparables

AI-generated content pricing is trending in two directions:
- **Consumer-grade AI newsletters:** free, ad-supported (The Rundown model)
- **Enterprise-grade AI analysis:** $5,000-$20,000 per report (Rocket model)

Albis sits between — subscription-priced ($2k-$15k/year) AI-personalised intelligence. This is a legitimate mid-market position, but the floor is higher than pre-AI monitoring tools because AI-generated output has become a commodity at the low end. **This reinforces the case for raising Albis prices** — a $49/mo AI briefing reads as "ChatGPT output" in 2026, whereas $199-$499/mo reads as "curated intelligence service."

## 14. Revised Recommendation

The follow-up research confirms and sharpens the initial recommendation. The material changes:

### 14A. Pricing — confirmed, with one adjustment

Original recommendation holds: **$199 Starter / $499 Pro / $1,499 Company Intelligence monthly, annual-only**. The AI-era comparables reinforce this: free AI newsletters anchor the bottom, enterprise AI reports anchor the top, and a personalised-per-company subscription at $2-15k/year sits correctly in the middle.

**Adjustment:** Consider calling the tiers **"Solo / Team / Enterprise / Custom"** instead of "Pro / Team / Company Intelligence / Enterprise". The existing names double up on "Pro" and create confusion with Politico Pro, The Information Pro, etc.

### 14B. Positioning — new framing

Lead with three messages, in order:

1. **"Intelligence, not a dashboard"** — counters Meltwater/Cision/Talkwalker fatigue
2. **"Personalised to your company — read in 2 minutes"** — counters Stratfor/Oxford's generic long-form
3. **"Your strategy team's time is worth more than $5,000 a year"** — the ROI framing

### 14C. Go-to-market — sharpened

- **Self-serve checkout** on Starter and Pro (sales cycle < 30 days fits)
- **Dual path** on Company Intelligence (self-serve OR book a demo)
- **Enterprise sales motion only** at $25k+
- **Free Intelligence Preview** — one sample briefing, no login, captured lead
- **Replace free trial with 30-day money-back guarantee** on annual plans

### 14D. Onboarding — tiered

- **Starter:** fully automated, wizard + email sequence
- **Pro:** wizard + 15-min CS kickoff call
- **Company Intelligence:** wizard + 45-min strategy call + monthly CS for first quarter
- **Enterprise:** white-glove with named CSM

### 14E. Marketing claims backed by ROI math

Adopt the Dataminr template:
- Commission a Forrester-style ROI study once you have 5+ paying customers with outcome data
- Publish the study on the pricing page
- Quote specific numbers: time saved (hours/week), cost avoidance ($/year), decisions improved

### 14F. Build priorities aligned to pricing

To justify the recommended pricing, these product capabilities need to ship:

- **Reliable daily delivery** (already built in Phase 4)
- **Briefing feedback mechanism** (Phase 7.1) — proves product cares about quality
- **Explainability** (Phase 7.6) — "Why this story was selected" differentiates from generic AI
- **Weekly summary** (Phase 7.4) — tier differentiator for Pro+
- **Sample/preview briefing flow** — the critical lead magnet
- **Admin observability** (Phase 7.8) — required for enterprise security conversations
