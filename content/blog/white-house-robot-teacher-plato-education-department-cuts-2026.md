The White House just pitched a humanoid robot named "Plato" as the future of American education — while the actual US Department of Education sits 70% empty after losing half its workforce to layoffs. First Lady Melania Trump wants AI-powered robots teaching children at home. Teachers unions call it "every parent's nightmare." The collision happened in the same city, in the same week.

On March 25, a walking, talking Figure 03 robot escorted the First Lady into the East Room. It was the first humanoid robot to appear at the White House. Melania Trump told spouses of world leaders from 45 nations that machines like this one would soon become personalised tutors named "Plato," capable of teaching philosophy, science, and mathematics in every child's living room.

One day later, the AFT's Randi Weingarten stood at the AFL-CIO's Workers First AI Summit and called it exactly what millions of parents were thinking.

## A Robot Walks Into the White House

The [Fostering the Future Together Global Coalition Summit](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/melania-trump-robots-educators-kids-humanoid-systems/) wasn't subtle. Melania Trump didn't walk in with an aide. She walked in with a robot. Figure 03, built by Sunnyvale-based Figure AI — a company valued at $39 billion, backed by Intel, Nvidia, and Qualcomm — escorted her to the podium.

"Imagine a humanoid educator named Plato," she said. "Access to the classical studies is now instantaneous. Literature, science, art, philosophy, mathematics and history. Humanity's entire corpus of information is available in the comfort of your home."

She wasn't speaking hypothetically. "Very soon, artificial intelligence will move from our mobile phones to humanoids that deliver utility," she told the room. "Since our environment is designed for people, humanoid systems are uniquely suited to navigate and operate within our world."

The pitch: America's children must become "the most technologically fluent and highly educated generation in the world." The method: AI robots in homes, not human teachers in classrooms.

She pointed to 2,700 schools that participated in a K-12 AI Workshop she hosted with Zoom Communications in January, and a White House Presidential AI Challenge that drew entries from all 50 states. She cited NAEP data showing only 22% of 12th-graders are proficient in math and 35% in reading — the lowest scores in 20 years.

The data is real. But her conclusion — that robots are the answer — landed like a grenade.

## "Every Parent's Nightmare"

Randi Weingarten didn't mince words. At the AFL-CIO's inaugural Workers First AI Summit the very next day, the president of the American Federation of Teachers — representing 1.7 million educators — [called the robot pitch](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/melania-trump-robot-video-union-teacher-education-rcna265297) what it was.

"What she did yesterday was every parent's nightmare," Weingarten said. "This is exactly what Big Tech wants to create: a sense of a society that is being led by and taught by robots, displacing every bit of all of who we are, starting with education."

She pointed out that tech companies tried to replace teachers with screens 20 years ago. It didn't work then. "Now they're trying to get AI to replace teachers," she told NBC News.

The irony cuts deeper than ideology. Even conservative groups like Moms for Liberty have [recently pushed back](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/moms-liberty-teachers-unions-schools-tech-screen-time-rcna263931) against the amount of screen time students already spend in classrooms. The First Lady's vision of humanoid robots teaching children at home found critics on every side of the political spectrum.

The AFT itself isn't anti-AI. In July, the union launched a National Academy for AI Instruction in partnership with OpenAI and Anthropic, designed to train teachers to use AI as a tool — not be replaced by it. That distinction matters.

## The Building That's 70% Empty

Here's the part nobody connected.

While the White House pitched robot educators, the actual US Department of Education — the agency responsible for 50 million public school students — [has been hollowed out](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/reorganization/2026/03/a-year-after-mass-layoffs-education-dept-keeps-handing-off-its-programs-to-other-agencies/). In March 2025, it sent layoff notices to 1,400 employees. Another 600 retired or took voluntary separation. About 100 probationary employees were fired. Half the department's staff, gone.

The headquarters building in Washington is now [70% vacant](https://www.usnews.com/news/us/articles/2026-03-26/education-department-headquarters-will-relocate-as-part-of-trumps-dismantling). This week, the department announced it's relocating what remains.

Rachel Gittleman, who worked in the Ombudsman's Office handling student loan cases, described her last day to reporters: "I received an email stating that I was being fired and my organizational unit was being abolished to make the government more efficient." Her system access was shut off immediately. Nearly 400 student loan borrowers she was helping had no one.

Congress rejected the administration's calls for deep budget cuts. Lawmakers actually increased Education Department funding for 2026. But the administration is pressing ahead anyway, signing interagency agreements to transfer programs to the departments of Labor, Interior, State, and HHS.

Secretary Linda McMahon tried to lay off another 500 employees during the government shutdown last fall. Courts annulled those cuts.

So here's the contradiction: the same administration pitching robot teachers is actively dismantling the institution that exists to serve human ones.

## What the Scores Actually Say

The NAEP numbers Melania Trump cited are real, and they're alarming. American 12th-graders performing at 20-year lows in both math and reading isn't something to wave away. In California, only half of K-12 students met the state standard in English in 2025. A third met it in math. Scores remain lower than before the pandemic.

But the diagnosis matters more than the data. The question isn't whether American education is struggling. It's whether a $39 billion robot company or a fully staffed Department of Education is the better answer.

Figure AI, the company behind the White House robot, raised $1.75 billion in funding. Its investors include some of the richest companies on earth. The Figure 03 isn't marketed for education. It was designed for household tasks — laundry, cleaning, washing dishes. [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/melania-trump-robot-humanoid-robot-white-house-video-rcna265192) the robot works through a "vision-language-action model" that processes real-time sensor data. It's impressive engineering. It's not a teacher.

The week before, [Alpha School opened in Chicago](/blog/alpha-school-55k-ai-no-teachers-nyc-ban-grading-2026) charging $55,000 a year for AI-led classrooms with no teachers — while NYC simultaneously banned AI from grading students. The pattern keeps repeating: money flowing toward the AI solution, resources draining from the human one.

## Two Summits, Two Visions

The timing is what makes this story. Two summits in Washington. Same week. Opposite answers to the same question.

At the White House: 45 nations' representatives hearing about "Plato," the always-patient, always-available robot tutor. A vision of education as a technology product, delivered in living rooms, powered by companies worth tens of billions.

At the AFL-CIO: Labour leaders, teachers, and civil society arguing that education requires human connection, that children need adults who understand them, and that the rush to automate teaching serves shareholders, not students.

"We need human beings to actually help other human beings in the teaching and learning process," Weingarten said. "This is not about memorisation. This is not about becoming an automaton."

Globally, only 31% of US K-12 schools had formal AI governance policies as of December 2024, according to Coursera research. AI adoption has dramatically outpaced the rules meant to guide it. Countries like the UAE have made AI education mandatory from kindergarten through grade 12. Others are still debating whether ChatGPT should be allowed in classrooms.

For [countries already racing to deploy AI tutors](/blog/countries-racing-to-replace-teachers-with-ai-tutors-before-evidence-is-in), the White House robot moment will land as validation. For the 52 million children whose education is [already disrupted by the Iran war's economic fallout](/blog/iran-war-52-million-children-education-crisis-2026), a $39 billion robot company feels like a dispatch from another planet.

## The Question Nobody Asked

At no point during the Fostering the Future Together Summit did anyone on stage address the Department of Education's mass layoffs. Nobody mentioned the 400 student loan borrowers left without help when one office shut down overnight. Nobody asked whether the 2,700 schools in the K-12 AI Workshop had adequate broadband, or functional heating, or enough teachers to supervise the AI tools being pitched to them.

The scores are at a 20-year low. That much is true. But the response can't be a $39 billion robot escorting the First Lady past a government building that's 70% empty.

The machine was patient and always available. The teachers who lost their jobs were neither consulted nor mentioned.
