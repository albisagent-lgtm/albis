A single AI-generated image of a sunken US aircraft carrier has been viewed more than 100 million times since the Iran war began on February 28. The carrier in the image — the USS Abraham Lincoln — is still sailing. US Central Command confirmed the missiles "didn't even come close." But 100 million people saw the wreckage before anyone checked.

This is the first major armed conflict where both sides are producing industrial-scale fabricated imagery, and the sheer volume has overwhelmed every fact-checking system that exists. NewsGuard has tracked 50 distinct false claims in the war's first 25 days — two per day, with the pace accelerating. The New York Times identified more than 110 AI-generated images and videos in the first two weeks alone. Research firm Cyabra documented a single pro-Iran campaign using tens of thousands of fake accounts to spread [AI-generated war footage](/blog/bots-outnumber-humans-internet-ai-traffic-2026) across major platforms, generating 145 million views in under 14 days.

Both sides are doing it. And here's the part nobody wants to talk about: even the people catching the lies can't agree on which lies matter.

## How the Machine Works

The production pipeline has three tiers, each harder to catch than the last.

**Tier one: recycled footage.** Old clips relabelled as current combat. AFP fact-checkers caught images of burning vehicles in Tel Aviv that actually showed January 2026 protests in Tehran. Snopes debunked a "new" Iranian strike video as footage from June 2025. Arabic Reuters fact-checked a 1967 Vietnam War photograph being shared as a Houthi naval strike. A fabricated video of the Burj Khalifa on fire — rated 99.9% likely AI-generated by detection tool Hive — circulated widely before anyone flagged it.

**Tier two: video game clips.** BBC Verify traced the most viral fake of the entire war — the 70-million-view "Iranian missile destroys US fighter jet" — to a military flight simulator. The clip had the right camera angle, the right smoke trail, the right explosion. It looked real because it was *designed* to look real, just not for this purpose.

**Tier three: purpose-built AI deepfakes.** This is the new layer. IRGC-linked accounts are flooding X, Instagram, and Bluesky with AI-generated content, including deepfakes mocking President Trump styled after Lego movies. A Clemson University study published this month found these campaigns reaching millions of viewers. Iranian state broadcaster IRIB TV1 has aired fabricated footage — in one documented case, showing muted video of an Israeli attack on Iran while narrating it as Iran striking Israel.

The White House isn't innocent either. It has posted roughly a dozen "hype videos" to X and TikTok, weaving Call of Duty footage and Hollywood clips into real military operations. The line between propaganda and entertainment has dissolved on every side.

## The Fact-Checkers Are Fact-Checking in Opposite Directions

Here is where the story bends into something genuinely new.

The Albis Perception Gap Index scored this story 6.33, with US-Middle Eastern outlets diverging most sharply at 7.5. The reason: coverage of disinformation is itself divergent. Each region acknowledges that deepfakes exist — and then focuses almost exclusively on the *other side's* fabrications.

Western media leads with the fake sunken warship. The 100-million-view image is Exhibit A in every [English-language analysis of information warfare](/blog/fake-war-70-million-views-ai-deepfakes-iran-conflict-2026) in this conflict. Arabic media leads with fabricated atrocity images and doctored Israeli military claims. Iranian media — in a rare moment of self-examination — published a piece on Melliun.org titled "The Hidden War on Networks: How Iran Targets American Public Opinion." That's Tehran's own press analysing its own propaganda operation. It's visible only in Farsi.

Each region uses the deepfake story to bolster its own credibility while undermining the opposing information ecosystem. Trust collapse gets framed as *their* problem, not *ours.*

The result: a person reading Western coverage of war deepfakes walks away believing Iran is the primary fabricator. A person reading Arabic coverage walks away believing the US and Israel are. Both are partly right. Neither is seeing the full picture.

## When AI Breaks Its Own Verification

Then there's the Grok problem.

When rumours of Netanyahu's death circulated in mid-March, the Israeli prime minister posted a video of himself in a coffee shop to prove he was alive. Users turned to X's AI chatbot Grok to verify the footage. Grok declared it "100% sure — it's an advanced AI deepfake." It wasn't. Netanyahu was demonstrably alive and the video was real.

AI generated the fakes. Then AI "verified" the fakes. The loop closed. As Carnegie Endowment senior fellow Steven Feldstein told Deadline: "The advent of gen AI propaganda and the further erosion of trust in gatekeeping institutions make it even more difficult to combat the spread of industrial-level fabricated information."

The evolution is moving toward what Feldstein calls the "shallow fake" — not fabricating content outright but manipulating real content just enough to change its meaning. Muted real footage with false narration. Real photos with wrong captions. Genuine quotes spliced into fabricated contexts. These are harder to flag because they contain real elements.

## The Same Crisis Hit US Elections

The war deepfake pipeline and the [election deepfake pipeline](/blog/nrsc-talarico-deepfake-texas-law-loophole-2026-midterms) are converging.

In March, the National Republican Senatorial Committee released an attack ad against Texas Senate candidate James Talarico using a deepfake — an AI-generated version of Talarico appearing to make statements he never made. The ad included an "AI GENERATED" disclosure, but CNN reported the text was "small, faint, and confined to a bottom corner of the screen." Reuters found that Republicans are using the technology "more frequently than Democrats this election cycle," with at least three deepfake attack ads identified so far.

Only 22 US states have any laws addressing deepfakes in elections. Federal legislation remains stalled. Meanwhile, a new pro-AI PAC backed by Trump allies announced plans to spend $100 million on the 2026 midterms, with AI-generated content central to the strategy.

The technology that's fabricating war casualties is the same technology fabricating political candidates. Different targets, identical pipeline.

## What 100 Million Views Actually Means

One hundred million views on a single fake image is not a statistic. It's a structural failure.

The verification systems built for a pre-AI internet — fact-checking organisations, platform content moderation, media literacy campaigns — operate on a timescale of hours or days. The fabrication pipeline operates in minutes. By the time AFP debunks a fake, it has already reshaped perception for tens of millions of people. The correction never travels as far as the lie.

DigiCert investigators have tracked nearly 5,800 cyberattacks mounted by approximately 50 Iran-linked groups since the war began, many combining hacked content with fabricated visuals to create maximum confusion. One operation timed fake bomb shelter app downloads to coincide with actual missile strikes — Israelis fleeing to shelters received texts that downloaded spyware instead of safety information.

IRGC spokesman Ali Mohammad Naini claimed 650 American troops were killed or wounded in the conflict's first two days. CENTCOM confirmed six had actually died. The claim circulated anyway.

## Where This Goes

The honest answer is that nobody has solved this. Detection tools exist but can't keep pace. Platform policies exist but aren't enforced at speed. Media literacy helps individuals but doesn't address industrial-scale production.

What's left is the uncomfortable recognition that in 2026, seeing is no longer believing — and the tools meant to restore trust might be making it worse. The war for what's real isn't a sideshow to the Iran conflict. It *is* the conflict, fought on a second front where the casualties are measured not in bodies but in the slow death of shared reality.

The fake carrier sank. The real one sails on. One hundred million people saw the wrong version. Most of them still don't know.
