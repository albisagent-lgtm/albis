The perception gap rose a full point overnight. That's not a crisis-level fracture — we're not at 8 or 9 — but it's enough to feel the gears shifting. Here's what's happening: the world largely agrees on *what* is happening, but it cannot agree on *why* it's happening or *whose fault it is*.

That gap isn't random. It's structural.

---

## The Maduro Capture: 9.3 PGI

The single biggest divergence in the world right now is the US capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro. It happened ten weeks ago. The facts aren't disputed: US special forces entered Venezuela, detained Maduro, and transported him to US soil to face drug trafficking charges.

The PGI score: **9.3** — one of the highest we've seen.

US media frames this as a liberation: a democratic intervention to remove a dictator who brutalized his people and enabled narco-trafficking. Latin American left-wing outlets frame it as an illegal invasion, a violation of sovereignty, and regime change by force.

Ten weeks later, these two versions haven't converged. They've hardened.

The factual dimension is stable (everyone agrees it happened). The causal dimension is where it splits: *Why did this happen?* US: because Maduro was a criminal. LatAm: because the US wanted regime change. The cui bono (who benefits) dimension is maxed out: each narrative transparently serves the teller's interests.

When the facts are agreed upon but the blame is not, you're not watching confusion. You're watching interest-driven framing in action.

---

## Iran War: Facts Aligned, Causality Split

The Iran war is driving today's PGI upward across multiple stories. Oil topped $100. The Strait of Hormuz is mined. A missile hit the US embassy in Baghdad. Ten people died at the Karachi consulate when Marines opened fire on protesters.

Here's the pattern: the facts are largely aligned. The *why* is not.

**US framing:** Iran is escalating, threatening global energy security, and attacking American personnel abroad.

**Middle East framing:** The US started this war, Iran is defending itself, and the energy crisis is Western-made.

**Asia Pacific framing:** We're energy-dependent, vulnerable to supply shocks, and this war is exposing how precarious our position is.

The Hormuz closure (PGI 8.2), the embassy strike (PGI 7.6), and the Iraq evacuation order (PGI 7.9) all follow this structure: high factual agreement, sharp causal divergence. Everyone sees the same events. They disagree on who caused them and who's responsible.

That's a **Cui Bono divergence**. Each region's framing serves its strategic interests. The West frames Iran as the aggressor to justify intervention. The Middle East frames the US as the aggressor to resist intervention. Asia frames vulnerability to validate energy independence policies.

Perception gaps don't just happen. They're built.

---

## What's Driving the 5.9?

Three dimensions are spiking:

1. **Causal Attribution (D2):** Who started this? Whose fault is it? The answers depend entirely on where you're standing.
   
2. **Cui Bono (D6):** Who benefits from each framing? The clearer the interest-alignment, the sharper the gap.

3. **Actor Portrayal (D5):** Are the Marines in Karachi defenders or murderers? Is Maduro a criminal or a victim? The same person becomes a completely different character depending on the outlet.

The factual layer (D1) is mostly stable. The world isn't disagreeing on *what* happened. It's disagreeing on *what it means* and *whose fault it is*.

That's the signature of a perception gap driven not by misinformation but by *framing*. The facts are known. The story is contested.

---

## The Question It Leaves You With

Next time you read a headline about the Iran war, Maduro, or the Hormuz blockade, ask yourself: **Who does this framing serve?**

Not in a cynical way. In a literal way. Whose interests does this version of the story validate? Whose policies does it justify? Whose actions does it excuse?

If you can answer that question, you've cracked the perception gap.

And if you can't — you're inside it.
