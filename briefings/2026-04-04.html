ALBIS April 4, 2026

Iran shot down a US F-15E — the first American aircraft lost in five weeks of war — as Iranian missiles struck a Kuwait desalination plant that supplies drinking water to millions.

The Iran war is no longer a military operation with economic side effects — it is now a direct assault on the systems that keep civilians alive, from water and fuel to food and medicine, across four continents.

The Big Story

→ Iran's air defenses downed a US F-15E Strike Eagle, forcing a crew rescue and raising the cost calculus for the Pentagon's five-week air campaign.

→ Iranian missiles hit a Kuwait desalination plant and oil refinery, attacking the water supply for a nation that depends on desalination for nearly all its drinking water.

→ A UK-led coalition of 40 nations pledged action to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, where daily ship transits have collapsed from 130 to single digits, choking 20% of the world's oil supply.

→ The EU moved toward fuel rationing as the war entered its second month, with Slovenia already limiting purchases at the pump and Lithuania halving train fares to cut driving.

The pattern across today's headlines: the war has moved from destroying military targets to destroying civilian infrastructure on both sides — hospitals, bridges, water plants, refineries — and every hit ripples outward through systems that feed, fuel, and hydrate populations far from the battlefield.

Around the World

→ Trump signed a 100% tariff on imported brand-name pharmaceuticals, starting at 20% now and escalating to full rate by 2030, threatening drug supply chains built over decades.

→ SpaceX filed for an IPO targeting a valuation above $2 trillion, which would make it the largest stock market listing in history and concentrate unprecedented wealth in a single private company.

→ Samsung expects a sixfold jump in Q1 profit to a record 50 trillion won, driven entirely by AI memory chip demand even as Google's TurboQuant wiped $100 billion off rival chipmakers this week.

→ Pakistan mandated a four-day work week and moved all university classes online to conserve energy, while Bangladesh cut office hours to 9–4 and froze new vehicle purchases.

→ Sudan's famine worsened as the civil war neared its third year, with Hormuz-driven supply disruptions compounding mass starvation and MSF reporting 3,396 survivors of sexual violence treated in Darfur clinics alone.

→ ESCWA warned that 5 million more Arabs face food insecurity as war-driven price rises of 20% push the region's poorest households past the hunger threshold.

→ Turkey's inflation hit 30.87% year-on-year with energy price hikes looming, while Venezuela's bolívar continued its collapse under 600% annual inflation.

→ Cape Town dam levels dropped to 47.8% — twenty percentage points below last year — putting 4.7 million residents on notice for water restrictions as the dry season begins.

→ China's domestic chip makers captured 41% of the AI server market, a share that US export controls paradoxically accelerated, prompting a bipartisan MATCH Act to ban chipmaking equipment sales entirely.

→ Artemis II astronauts tested the Orion spacecraft en route to the Moon, on track for Monday's lunar flyby — the first humans beyond low Earth orbit since 1972.

What You Didn't See

Stories in other languages that never reached English media:

→ Arabic — Gulf media led with Iran's strikes on Kuwait civilian infrastructure as the top story, framing the war as an attack on Arab water security — a narrative absent from English-language coverage, which led with the F-15 shootdown.

→ Hindi — Indian media focused on the Reserve Bank of India freezing its rate-cut cycle as war-driven oil prices stall the recovery for 1.4 billion people — a domestic economic crisis barely registered in Western outlets.

→ Korean — South Korean coverage centred on Google's TurboQuant memory-efficiency breakthrough cratering Samsung and SK Hynix shares, framed as a national security threat to Korea's semiconductor economy.

→ Russian — State media celebrated billions in windfall oil revenue from the Iran war while framing Europe's energy crisis as self-inflicted — the same conflict producing Russian profits and European rationing.

One Thing to Watch

Europe has roughly six days to get fertilizer to farms before the mid-April spring planting deadline passes. If the Hormuz closure and fuel rationing prevent adequate inputs from reaching fields this week, an autumn harvest shortfall across the continent becomes locked in — and no amount of diplomacy after the fact can undo a missed growing season.

Today's Human Moment

Somewhere above the Pacific, four Artemis II astronauts are running systems checks on the Orion spacecraft as they sail toward Monday's lunar flyby — the first humans to travel beyond low Earth orbit since Gene Cernan left the Moon in December 1972. Fifty-four years later, a species busy tearing apart its own infrastructure on the ground is still reaching for the Moon. That stubbornness might be our best quality.
